  'missing one or more' counts openings that left at least one of the seat's own
  datasets uncited; 'citing none' counts those that cited none of them. The planted seat's
  opening in a planted run is the harness's script, not the room's, so it is left out of both.

  A seat is measured on its OPENING only - the one turn written without seeing the
  others', where citing none of your own part means your part went unread. A reply that
  reaches for another seat's dataset is the debate working, not a gap. An absent seat has
  no opening and is not counted, which is why openings is printed beside the share
  instead of the roster size. A ruling with no grounds is the Chair naming a fault line
  and then not saying what decided it; which WAY it ruled is the Chair's call and is not
  counted here.

  PRIMARY is six advisors against four, on identical packs and identical questions.
  Read it differently from the rest, on purpose:
    - a DIFFERENCE (p < 0.05) is a reason NOT to drop two seats;
    - no measurable difference is NOT proof that four match six. It is a failure to
      detect one at this sample size, and the sample is small by design. An equivalence
      claim needs a margin fixed in advance and far more runs than this plan buys.
      What this plan can do is rule out a LARGE loss from dropping two seats.
  A vs B and D vs B ask whether either room beats the Chairman at all. B is the

  beside the efficiency table above, which prices each correct answer.
  A vs E, if E was run, is the architecture question: if E matches A, the second pass is
  doing the work rather than the seats.
